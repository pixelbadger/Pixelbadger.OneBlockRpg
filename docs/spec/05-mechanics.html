5 · Mechanics: SPECIAL

The RPG system. Every number the engine rolls against lives here. It applies identically to the player and every character.

Baseline: the formulas below are the classic Fallout 2 values, adopted as a starting point and expected to be tuned. "SPECIAL" is a Bethesda/ZeniMax trademark, so use the name as internal shorthand only and choose a public name before any release.

5.1 Attributes

Seven attributes, each 1–10. A typical person is 5.

Characters' SPECIAL is authored in the payload. open Q26: is the player character fixed by the payload, or created with Fallout's point-buy (every attribute starts at 5, plus 5 free points, all within 1–10)?

5.3 Skills

Skills are percentages derived from attributes. Three tag skills per character get +20 and gain double from skill points. The core set trims Fallout's list to what suits a city block. A payload can enable more, for example Big Guns for a different setting.

5.4 Checks

  • Skill check: roll d100 (seeded) ≤ skill + tier modifier. Tiers: trivial +30, easy +15, normal 0, hard −15, very hard −30, heroic −50. The margin is reported, so effects can distinguish a narrow pass from a clear pass.
  • Attribute check: roll d10 ≤ attribute + modifier. Used for raw tests such as forcing a door (ST), spotting something (PE), or holding liquor (EN).
  • Opposed check: actor's skill roll against a defender value: Sneak vs 10×PE of observers, Steal vs 10×PE of the target (−1 per kg of the item), Speech vs the target's relationship trust plus 5×IN.
  • Who may request a check: engine rules, authored options, hooks and use rules, LLM-suggested options (tier only, §6.3), and the director's allowed moves (§6.7). The engine always resolves the roll. The LLM never decides an outcome.

5.5 Action points and time

AP is the bridge between turn-based combat and the exploration clock:

  • In combat, each round grants full AP, and actions spend it (5.6). A round is 10 game seconds.
  • Outside combat, every action has a base time in minutes, scaled by 8 / AP. That makes AP 8 neutral (AG 6–7), AP 5 1.6× slower and AP 10 0.8×. Agile characters get more done in a day.

5.6 Combat

Combat begins whenever an attack action resolves: in exploration, in conversation (§6.6) or in a set piece (§6.7).

  1. Combatants: attacker and target, plus anyone in the room whose behaviour rules or relationships make them join or flee.
  2. Order: by Sequence, then AG, then a seeded roll.
  3. Turns: each combatant spends AP: unarmed 3, melee 3–4, firearm 4–5 (per weapon), aimed shot +1, reload 2, use or equip item 2, leave the room 4 (which exits combat unless pursued), bark 0.
  4. Hit chance = weapon skill − target AC + weapon accuracy − 20 per point of ST below the weapon's minimum, clamped to 5–95%.
  5. Critical: a hit roll ≤ critical chance doubles damage and ignores DT. Crit tables are deferred.
  6. Damage = (weapon roll + melee damage bonus for melee/unarmed − armour DT) × (1 − armour DR%), minimum 0.
  7. Down: at HP ≤ 0 a character is unconscious, and at HP ≤ −EN dead. Characters the payload marks essential are only ever knocked out, which protects the authored story. open Q27: what happens when the player is downed?
  8. NPC decisions are scripted. Each character has a combat profile (aggressive, defensive, coward, flee at X% HP, preferred weapon). There are no LLM calls per round. Inside a set piece the director may override targets and intentions.
  9. Range: everyone in the same room can be targeted. open Q24: may ranged weapons fire through an open exit into an adjacent room?
  10. Text: hit, miss, crit and down messages are authored templates per weapon type. All combat time is added to the clock when combat ends.

5.7 Items, sharing and trade

KindMechanical fields
Weaponskill, damage min–max, AP cost, accuracy, min ST, melee/ranged, ammo type and capacity
ArmourAC bonus, DT, DR%
Consumableeffects: heal HP, reduce fatigue, timed attribute or skill modifiers
Any itemmass (kg), value
  • Give is voluntary and needs the item to be held. Take from a character needs consent: a give from them, a successful Steal check, or the character being down.
  • Steal is an opposed check (5.4). If caught, the theft is witnessed: the target's trust drops and the event enters their memory and day log.
  • Trade: buy price = value × (1 + (merchant Barter − buyer Barter) / 200), clamped to 0.5×–2×. Sell price is the mirror. Money is a numeric character property.

5.8 Fatigue, sleep and healing

  • Fatigue is hours awake. Past waking hours (14 + EN): −1 AG and −1 PE per extra hour (minimum 1). At waking hours + 4, the character collapses and sleeps where they stand.
  • Sleep is an action on anything with the sleepable affordance (bed, sofa), or on the floor with a penalty (only half the fatigue recovered). Sleep lasts 8 hours or until a trigger wakes the sleeper.
  • The player's sleep ends the day. It is the summarisation point for day-based memory (§6.8).
  • Healing: Healing rate per 6 hours, doubled while asleep. First Aid (once per day per wound) and Doctor checks heal immediately.
  • NPCs obey the same rules. Their schedules include sleep.

5.9 Progression

  • XP comes mainly from authored awards (an award_xp effect on triggers, hooks and set-piece outcomes), plus small amounts for successful checks and won fights. The payload controls the economy.
  • Levels follow Fallout's curve: level n needs n(n−1)/2 × 1000 XP. Each level grants 5 + 2×IN skill points and 2 + ⌊EN/2⌋ HP.
  • open Q25: perks and traits. They are deferred for now, and a one-block game will have few levels.

5.10 What the engine core must implement

  • SPECIAL, derived statistics and skills as pure functions, recomputed when attributes, equipment or modifiers change
  • A seeded check resolver (skill, attribute, opposed) returning pass/fail plus margin
  • AP-based combat with sequence, hit, crit, damage, down/death, combat profiles and authored message templates
  • Item kinds (weapon, armour, consumable), equipment slots, carry capacity
  • Give / steal / trade, with witnesses
  • Fatigue, sleep, healing, and the day boundary
  • XP, levels, skill points, tag skills
  • Timed modifiers (consumables, fatigue penalties)
